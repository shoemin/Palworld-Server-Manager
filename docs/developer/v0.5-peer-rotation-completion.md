# Peer credential promotion and staging lapse

Trial reference: #44k, within #44. These are the receiving Host's completion rules for a credential it already durably staged and verified. [Durable proposal ordering and receiver staging](v0.5-rotation-staging.md) now supply the persistence foundation. The rotating Host's global cutover/retirement engine remain later work. No new trust or permissions are created here.

`ObserveActivePeerCredential` accepts only actual completed mutual-TLS evidence supplied by trusted Host transport. It rechecks Active trust, recovery state and the exact current or pending fingerprint inside its transaction. A live presentation of Pending promotes that key even after its staging deadline: Current becomes New, Old's fingerprint/time are appended to credential history, and the pending fingerprint/deadline/reconfirmation flag are cleared. History never stores a RotationId. Promotion and its authenticated-peer audit commit together. A failed audit rolls both back. Repeated/concurrent observations append one history record and one audit; future authentication rejects Old, including later calls on an existing old-key connection.

The exact `PendingRotationId` survives promotion until the rotating Host confirms durable receipt of the promotion acknowledgement. A reopened repository can therefore reconstruct the same acknowledgement without relying on in-memory state or credential history. `ConfirmPeerRotationReceipt` clears that ID only for the exact authenticated current peer and matching retained rotation, after its caller has received that durable receipt. It cannot promote a pending key. Once cleared, replay does nothing; a receipt naming a different currently-retained rotation is refused. This persistence primitive does not implement or claim delivery of the future receipt RPC.

Expiry retains both already-verified pins and keeps the relationship Active. The owned trust journal's startup/periodic maintenance marks `PendingReconfirmationRequired` with a system-attributed audit, atomically; an Old-authenticated live call also performs this check. The deadline is compared as an instant, including exact equality. Neither expiry nor Old presentation promotes or extends a deadline. Old remains usable until actual New presentation; the retained New remains recognizable live after lapse. Proposal/reconfirmation policy must respect this durable flag and independently check the deadline before accepting any refresh. An Old-authenticated status response claiming CutOver or Completed must never substitute for actual New presentation.

Certificate admission is deliberately read-only because TLS invokes it before private-key possession proof completes. The outbound pinned transport exposes a separate public observation callback after completed mutual TLS, negotiated HTTP/2 and actual local/remote certificate validation, before releasing the HTTP stream. The Host's activation client uses that callback for observation; inbound per-RPC authentication uses the completed connection evidence. A certificate that was merely advertised can never promote trust. Ordinary operation authorization remains the separate #45 gate.

Five ordinary scenarios exercise concurrent promotion, retained receipt after reopen, exact receipt identity, grant preservation, expiry and all three audit rollback boundaries, recovery/inactive/malformed refusal, real mutual-TLS New presentation and Old rejection, and the real pinned gRPC client's post-handshake observation. A deliberately incomplete transport invokes certificate admission without completing TLS and verifies no promotion occurs. Staged rows are synthetic accepted-stage inputs; actual sockets/key proof are real. This is not complete proposal/cutover, process-kill/reboot or multi-machine qualification. Full #44 remains incomplete.
