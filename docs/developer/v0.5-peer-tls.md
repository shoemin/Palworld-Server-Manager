# Pinned peer TLS profile

Trial reference: #44d, within #44. `WindowsPeerTls` supplies the Host-side TLS profile used by the [peer gRPC activation seam](v0.5-peer-grpc.md) and separate [first-contact PAKE stream](v0.5-peer-pairing.md). The existing Host startup does not open a new listener. The [durable activation seam](v0.5-peer-activation.md) is available. Rotation mutation and local authorization/service composition remain subsequent work.

Both endpoints present an ECDSA P-256 certificate and prove possession of its private key through TLS. Peer validation checks certificate validity dates and the SHA-256 fingerprint of its SubjectPublicKeyInfo against the trusted caller's pin resolver. DNS names, certificate subjects and public CA membership do not replace the stored Host-key pin. Chain/CRL checking is not the trust source for these self-signed identity certificates. Invalid/missing certificates, another curve, unknown pins or a failing resolver are rejected. The resolver must use authenticated trust metadata, never request-provided candidate pins.

TLS 1.2/1.3 and HTTP/2 ALPN are explicit. Renegotiation and session resumption are disabled. The provided stream helpers impose a ten-second handshake deadline, require mutual authentication and HTTP/2 after the handshake, and close the transferred transport on failure or cancellation. Successful callers own and dispose the returned stream. Certificate handles are borrowed from Host-owned credential loading and must outlive their connections; no private key is exported or persisted by this profile. A later gRPC adapter using the options directly must retain the same timeout, ownership and post-handshake requirements.

The certificate-validation callback is only a handshake check; it is not itself proof that the handshake finished. Use identity evidence only after successful mutual TLS completion. Similarly, completing TLS creates no capability grant.

`PeerTransportAuthentication` reads the current Host-owned trust row on every call and matches the actual TLS fingerprint to the requested peer UUID. An unexpired PeerBound with verified local-binding metadata permits only pairing-finalization traffic. Ordinary management traffic requires Active trust, followed by the separate #45 authorization checks. Unknown purposes, missing/revoked peers and recovery-required relationships are rejected. This component never executes an operation, and #45 must also recheck trust inside its authorization/mutation transaction rather than treat a returned identity object as authority.

An already-authenticated pending rotation pin remains recognized for live TLS even when its staging window has lapsed, as required by §4a-i. `PendingReconfirmationRequired` gates refreshing that staging, not recognition of a recorded key. The returned identity distinguishes pending from current credentials for the later rotation observer. An unapproved replacement candidate is not a trusted pending rotation key. Recovery-completion traffic will need its own bounded proof flow; it is not admitted by the ordinary finalization purpose here.

Three ordinary test scenarios exercise actual Windows TLS over temporary loopback sockets with isolated synthetic CNG credentials: reciprocal proof and HTTP/2; rejection of wrong pins, resolver failure, missing client proof, ALPN mismatch, unusable credentials and cancellation/cleanup; and database-backed per-call trust-purpose checks. Negative handshake tests cannot pass merely by reaching their timeout. Tests delete their own named credential containers. This is actual TLS qualification, not a real multi-machine gRPC pairing or deployed Host result. Run the ordinary suite or `--peer-tls-probe`. Full CI and two-pass review records are in the trial ledger.
