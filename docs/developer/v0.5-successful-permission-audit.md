# Successful permission audit integrity

Trial reference: #45l. Component review found that accepted grant/default writers checked authority effects after auditing but did not verify the inserted audit row itself. A controlled SQLite trigger deleting a successful grant's audit reproduced a committed grant without its required record at the PR146 checkpoint. This is an escaped defect originating in the #45b success writer and inherited by later canonical batches, not a new product decision or an ordinary client ability to install database triggers.

The private WriteSuccessAudit helper records the existing controlled fields and returns a validator tied to that same connection and transaction. The validator checks the generated audit identity, time, event kind, actual actor kind and nullable local/peer identities, exact Host/server target, offline flag and complete controlled summary. Missing or altered rows fail with InvalidOperationException and the enclosing transaction rolls back. No audit schema, event meaning or authority rule changes.

All local/remote direct Host/server grants, local/remote presets, current-Owner historical reissue, creator finalization, Owner subtree invalidation and default configuration validate their successful audit records after the final authority/effect checks and before cancellation/commit. Batch writers collect every validator and check the whole batch after all inserts. Active-default application includes its validators in the existing final callback after the enclosing PeerActivated audit. Checking immediately after each insert would leave earlier rows exposed to later audit-trigger changes.

The audit validator itself performs only a SELECT, holds no global state and creates no denial event. Existing current-proof, revision, Owner, exact target, canonical provenance, rollback and cancellation checks remain required. Pre-write policy denials retain their separate denial-only transaction; later success-audit failures never become permission denials. This does not repair historical audit rows or change another security subsystem's audit writer.

Four regression scenarios include the original failing deletion reproduction; 13 successful action paths times 11 deletion/field mutations (143 cases); 12 later-batch/enclosing-audit deletion or modification cases; and eight concurrent preset attempts with one revision winner and only its two audit rows. Each case in the two fault matrices compares the persisted permission/default/trust/inventory/revision/audit snapshot before and after refusal, then removes the trigger and proves retry succeeds. Fixtures use controlled trusted identity evidence and actual SQLite transactions. They do not claim an exposed permission RPC, physical multi-machine execution or actual Palworld creation.

The [component acceptance inventory](v0.5-authorization-component-acceptance.md) records the corrected engine qualification and downstream boundaries. Accessibility certification is WAIVED, never PASS; physical Windows parity, Palworld, Linux and release gates remain downstream.
